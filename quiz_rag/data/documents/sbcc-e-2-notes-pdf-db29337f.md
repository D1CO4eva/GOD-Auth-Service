Srimad Bhagavatam is here!

Srimad Bhagavatam seems to be at the bottom of the authority pyramid. If that is
so, then what is so special about it? Oh! It is very special! Read on!

The journey through our Sanatana Dharma’s philosophy rests on three main pillars:
1. Upanishads
2. Bhagavad Gita
3. Brahma Sutrās

Together, these three are referred to as ‘Prasthāna Traya’ .
Prasthāna means journey.
Traya, as you might have rightly guessed, is ‘set of three’.

Hence  Prasthāna  Traya  means  the  essential  three  for
Sanatana Dharma’s philosophical journey.

Pillar 1 : Upanishads

Upanishads are the very ‘fruit’ of Vedas.
A tree’s ultimate ‘purpose’ for humanity is generally
the  fruit,  right?  So  Upanishads  hold  an  important
place in the Vedas.
We  have  about  108  mainstream  Upanishads  from
all  four  branches  of  Vedas  (Rig,  Yajur,  Sāma,
Atharva). Of these, 10 occupy a prime spot.

Pillar 2 : Srimad Bhagavad Gita

Second pillar comes from the middle of the shown pyramid, i.e. Itihasas.
This  is  none  other  than  Srimad  Bhagavad  Gita,  the  divine  discourse/discussion
between Bhagavan and Sri Arjuna in Mahabharata.

Where can we ﬁnd Srimad Bhagavad Gita?
Mahabharata is a huge Itihasa with 18 sections (named as parvas)
The ﬁrst and last parva names are easy to remember:
First Section - Adi Parva - Literally means ‘ﬁrst section’!
Last (18th) section - Swarga-ārohana Parva - ‘Ascent to Heaven’ section.

Srimad Bhagavad Gita is in the 6th section called Bhishma Parva - Chapters 25-42.

Srimad Bhagavad Gita - Some Interesting Details:
• Total Verses (slokas) - 700
• Chapters adhyāyas - 18
• Number of names of Bhagavan mentioned: 18
   (eg., Hrshikesha, Krishna, Achyuta, etc.)

Sage Veda Vyasa gave us the nectarine words of Bhagavan graced to humanity at
large  in  the  battleﬁeld  at  Kurukshetra,  in  form  of  Srimad  Bhagavad  Gita.  It
integrates the paths of Karma Yoga, Raja Yoga, Bhakti Yoga and Gnana Yoga into a
unitary lovely fabric of wholeness.

Pillar 3 : Brahma Sutrās

Sage Veda Vyasa gave a synthesis of all Upanishads; harmonised them to show that
the message cut across all Upanishads is one and the same without any real conﬂict!
Sage Veda Vyasa gave this in the form of aphorisms (sutrās - short, distilled, concise
rules)

This is how Brahma Sutrās are organised:

4 chapters
16 sections
Many topics
Many Aphorisms  (called Sutrās & one or more Sutrās make an Adhikaraṇa)

(called Adhyāya)
(called Pādam & 4 pādams make an Adhyāya)
(called Adhikaraṇa & many Adhikaraṇas make a Pādam)

The number of sutrās vary from one tradition to another.
• Advaita tradition has 555 sutrās, 223 topics, 16 sections, 4 chapters.
• Vishishtadvaita (Sri Vaishnava) - 544(5) sutrās, 156 topics, 16 sections, 4

chapters.

• Dvaita (Sri Madhvacharya) - 564 sutrās, 223 topics, 16 sections, 4 chapters.

The  second  sutrā  is  “Janmadyasya  yatha:”,  which  incidentally  is  also  the  opening
phrase of Srimad Bhagavata Mahapurana.

Hence these 3 pillars form the foundation of Vedanta - the quest for the Supreme
Truth.
Pillar 1 : Upanishads - Vedas
Pillar 2 : Srimad Bhagavad Gita - Itihasas
Pillar 3 : Brahma Sutrās - By Sage Veda Vyasa

Now comes the interesting part:
Srimad  Bhagavatam  covers  all  these  foundational  pillars  by  being  the  essence/
expansion/purport of all these three.

What is the pramāṇā or proof for this?
Sage Veda Vyasa himself says (in Garuda Purana),

Now let us see Why Srimad Bhagavatam really is Great & Special:

Pillar 1: Upanishads (Vedas)
(gāyatrī-bhāṣya-rūpo'sau vedārtha-paribṛṁhitaḥ)
Srimad Bhagavatam is a detailed commentary of Gayatri mantra and is the very essence
of all Upanishads.
Pillar 2: Srimad Bhagavad Gita (Mahabharata’s essence)
(bhāratārtha-vinirṇayaḥ)
It is the essence of Sage Vyasa’s Mahabharata, and Srimad Bhagavatam is graced by the
very author of Mahabharata.
Pillar 3: Brahma Sutrās
(artho'yaṁ brahma-sūtrāṇāṁ)
Srimad Bhagavatam is the true meaning and commentary of the Brahma Sutrās, graced by
its very author Sage Veda Vyasa himself.

Other Puranas
(purāṇānāṁ sāma-rūpaḥ)
It is also the ‘sāma roopa' of Puranas - the essence & harmonized form of all Puranas!
Bhagavan Himself has graced this Purana!
(sākṣād-bhagavatoditaḥ)

As  our  Guru,  Maharanyam  Sri  Sri  Muralidhara  Swamiji,  sings  in  his
‘madhuragitam’  composition,  ‘Srimad  Bhagavatam  Svayam  Harireva’  -  “prasthāna
turiyam brahma vidyā poshakam”.

Some More Specialities of Srimad Bhagavatam:

• Sage Veda Vyasa also says, ‘purāṇam brahma sammitam’ (SB 1.3.40), which means
Srimad Bhagavatam is to be seen with the same reverence as one would see the
Vedas themselves. It is equivalent to the Vedas.

• ‘nigama kalpa taror galitam phalam’  (SB 1.1.3) - It is the fruit and essence of all

Vedas, in a form accessible to one and all.

Hence Srimad Bhagavatam, for all practical purposes, for bhāgāvatās like us, is
Sanatana Dharma; the essence of all scriptures!

Hence as lovers of our Dharma, it behoves us to learn Srimad Bhagavatam, the
very epitome & essence of Sanatana Dharma.

2.2 Srimad Bhagavatam Is Bhagavan Himself

Shastras  usually  show  the  way  to  attain  Bhagavan.  As  Srimad  Bhagavatam  is  the
essence of all shastras, it not only shows how to attain Bhagavan, but what makes it
unique  is  that  Srimad  Bhagavatam  is  Bhagavan  Himself!  This  is  outlined  in  the
Padma Purana.
