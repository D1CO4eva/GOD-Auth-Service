5.1 Deeper Dive Into Puranas
Three Types of Samhitas
1. The Vedas are known as ‘Rāja Samhita.’ The Vedas issue commands. They do
not coax or plead with us. They command us like a king, saying, “Speak the
truth; remain steadfast in dharma.” So, the Vedas are called the Rāja Samhita.
2. The Puranas are known as ‘Suhṛt Samhita.’ In Sanskrit, the word suhṛt means
‘friend.’ The Puranas explain spiritual truths to us in a friendly, kind, and
approachable manner—speaking in a way that can help us understand and
accept them. Hence they are called the Suhṛt Samhita. We already saw that
Puranas can be classified based on the three gunas: sāttvika, rājasa, and tāmasa.
Each Purana speaks in a manner suited to people of the corresponding
temperament and qualification. Srimad Bhagavatam is a sāttvika Purana. It
never threatens or intimidates. Instead, it presents the very same truths taught
by the Vedas in a delightful and appealing manner, thereby elevating us greatly.
3. Certain other sacred compositions are known as ‘Kāntā Samhita.’ These are
divinely inspired works of great poetic beauty, bestowed upon us by many
mahatmas. Just as a loving wife speaks gently, gracefully, and appropriately to
her husband, the Kāntā Samhita conveys spiritual truths with tenderness and
charm. Our Sri Swamiji’s Madhurageethams are Kāntā Samhitas graciously
bestowed especially for us.
What does ‘Purana’ mean?
‘Purana’ literally means ‘ancient’. It is sacred literature which is a compilation of
stories from ancient history. It covers a wide range of topics and presents abstract
concepts of the Vedas in an easily understandable form, through examples and
stories. There are 18 Puranas in all, as we saw earlier.

According to the Matsya Purana, five specific lakshaņās (characteristics or subjects,
in this context) need to be present for a scripture to qualify as a ‘Purana’1:
1. Sarga - Primary creation of ‘principles of the universe’ by Bhagavan
2. Pratisarga - Secondary creation of universe by Brahma
3. Vamsha - Genealogies of devas, rishis, and kings
4. Manvantara - Periods of rule of various ‘Manus’
5. Vamshānucharita - Dynastic history (solar and lunar dynasties)
5.2 Srimad Bhagavatam Is A Mahapurana
Srimad Bhagavatam is unique - it is called ‘Maha’-Purana. It has 10 lakshaņās - five
more than other Puranas. (These are delineated in canto 2, chapter 10 of Srimad
Bhagavatam2.)
Thus we see that there are ten subjects covered in Srimad Bhagavatam. So, are all
the ten principal subjects of the scripture?
No. ‘Ashraya’ is the sole principal (main) subject. The other nine are there only to
direct us towards ‘Ashraya’ - to purify us, so that we can ‘realize’ this subject fully!
Ashraya is Sri Krishna-the Brahman.

Cantos and Lakshaņās
Each canto of Srimad Bhagavatam covers dominant lakshaņā (characteristic/
subject). (Each canto may also have some fragments and fragrance of other
lakshaņās here and there, but it primarily holds one dominant lakshaņā.)
However, we see that there are 12 cantos, and only 10 lakshaņās. How do we
understand this? Below is a table listing the dominant lakshaņā in each canto, as
shown by our Guru Maharaj based on the commentary by Sri Sridharapāda3:
• Canto 1 - Preface (greatness of the listeners & speakers)
• Canto 2 - Introduction of the name of the text; Table of contents & a wonderful
summary of the whole text.
• Canto 3 - Sarga (primary creation of principles/building blocks)
• Canto 4 - Visarga (secondary creation of 8.4 million types of living organisms)
• Canto 5 - Sthānam (sustenance by Bhagavan’s will - through limits)
• Canto 6 - Poshanam (Bhagavan’s grace on devotees)
• Canto 7 - Ūthi(s) (Latent impressions or vāsanās)
• Canto 8 - Manvantara (the reign of various Manus and the dharma followed by
them)
• Canto 9 - Ishānukathā (stories of solar and lunar dynasties, of Bhagavan, and
bhāgavatas’ lives)
• Canto 10 - Ashraya (Krishna katha)
• Canto 11- Mukti (Liberation from cycles of birth)
• Canto 12 - Nirodha (souls withdrawing into Bhagavan, during dissolution, along
with their qualities, karmas, vāsanās, etc.)
This is how Srimad Bhagavatam’s cantos are divided and arranged.
Each canto will have many chapters which deal with a distinct subject, or a story or
portion of the story in line with the canto’s characteristic subject (lakshaņā).

5.3 Canto 1 - Part 1
Canto 1 serves as an introduction to Srimad Bhagavatam
It shows the greatness of the expounders & listeners of Srimad Bhagavatam:
• Narada Maharishi - Chapters 5,6
• Sri Veda Vyasa Bhagavan - Chapters 4,5
• Sri Shuka muni - Chapter 2,4
• Sri Parikshith Maharaj and his ancestors - Chapter 7-19
• Sri Ugrashravas Suta (Suta Pauranika) - Chapter 1

Some mahāns hence call it as the ‘Adhikāri Skandha’ - the skandha that speaks of
the apt and fit person to listen to and expound this grand Purana, in order to derive
the fruit of associating with this text by listening to or expounding it.

in order to please Bhagavan. In the interval between their rituals, Sri Suta

Shaunaka Rishi(s) asked six questions to Sri Suta Pauranika, to elicit the essence
of all scriptures leading to the lotus feet of Bhagavan. They asked:
1. What is the ultimate good for the people in this Kali Yuga?
2. Why did Bhagavan descend as the son of Devaki and Vasudeva?
3. Please describe the Gambheera leela of Bhagavan (His leela of Creation,
sustenance and dissolution of the universe).
4. Describe all his incarnations.
5. Describe the leelas Bhagavan did along with Balarama.
6. Where did Dharma take refuge after Bhagavan’s leela samvarana (departure)?
Srimad Bhagavatam contains the answers to these questions.
1. The greatest good for the people in Kali Yuga is Hari Bhakti (Devotion to Lord
Hari)
2. Bhagavan descended as the son of Devaki and Vasudeva to nourish devotion.
3. Gambheera Leela is discussed in Canto 2 & 3
4. Various Avataras/Incarnations are described in all skandhas/cantos.
• Varaha - Canto 3
• Matsya & Kurma - Canto 8
• Narasimha - Canto 7
• Vamana - Canto 8
• Parasurama & Sri Rama - Canto 9
• Balarama and Sri Krishna - Canto 10
• Kalki - Canto 12 & many others like Dattatreya, Kapila, Vyasa, Pruthu,
Buddha, etc. in various cantos.
5. The sports of Bhagavan along with Balarama is described in detail in 90
chapters of Canto 10,
6. In the age of Kali, Dharma has sought refuge in the Divine Names of the Lord.
