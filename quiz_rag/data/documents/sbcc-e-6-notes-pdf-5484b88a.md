6.1 Canto 1 Answers

Chapters 02 & 03
Sri Suta Pauranika was thrilled to have been chosen by the eminent sages for this
service.  After  paying  respects  to  Sri  Shuka,  Sri  Vyasa,  Lord  Narayana,  Nara  and
Devi Saraswati, he lauded the questions put forth by the sages.

That practice of supreme dharma alone, which gives rise to devotion in our hearts to
Hari as characterized by qualities such as an intense longing to listen to the stories
of  Sri  Krishna—that  alone  is  the  path  to  attaining  the  highest  good.  Practices  of
dharma that do not lead to such devotion are ultimately in vain.

The mundane outlook on life:
Dharma  →  Artha  (wealth)  →  Kāma  (pleasure)  →  Enjoyment  of  the  senses  →
More Dharma → More possessions …

The blissful ‘revolutionary’ path shown by the Srimad Bhagavatam:
Artha → Dharma → Bhakti → Jnāna → Ānanda
Kāma  Essential  Needs  →  only  to  sustain  the  body  in  which  the  Ātma  resides  →
strive to know the Tattva

What is that Tattva? Jnānis who know the Tattva declare:
The One Supreme Knowledge = Brahman = Paramātmā = Bhagavān!
How beautifully Srimad Bhagavatam brings these different perspectives of Divinity
together as One!
This  Supreme  Reality  can  be  attained  through  Bhakti  alone;  Satsanga,  the
association of holy devotees, awakens the desire for devotion.
Service to great souls + Listening to the divine stories of Vāsudeva → Puriﬁcation
of the heart → Bhakti

Sri  Suta  Pauranika  succinctly  replied  to  all  the  questions  covering  the  answers
pointed  above  and  described  all  the  avataras  of  the  Lord  too.  Finally  he  says  one
can ﬁnd the answers in detail to all these questions in the great Srimad Bhagavata
Purana which was composed by Sri Veda Vyasa.
Sri Veda Vyasa taught this Purana to his son Sri Shuka who in his turn related this
to King Parikshith, who was seated on the banks of the Ganga with a vow to fast
unto  death  (prāyopavesha).  Suta  Pauranika  said,  “I  was  one  among  the  many
fortunate  ones  who  listened  to  this  discourse  directly  from  Sri  Shuka,  and  I  shall
share it here with you according to best of my lights.”

6.2  Sage Narada & Sage Veda Vyasa’s Conversation

Chapter 04
Sri Shaunaka Rishi asked Suta Pauranika to expand on:
1. When, where, and why this discourse was held.
2. Who inspired Sri Veda Vyasa to compose this Purana.
3. How  did  Sri  Shuka,  who  was  an  enlightened  soul,  come  to  learn  this  Purana

from his father?

4. Why  did  King  Parikshith  quit  his  kingdom  and  take  a  vow  to  fast  unto  death

and how did he get to meet Sri Shuka and listen to the Purana from him?

Suta Pauranika started the story from the avatara of Sri Veda Vyasa:
In  the  Dwapara  Yuga,  Sage  Vyasa  was  born  to  Satyavati  and  Sage  Parashara.
Seated  in  meditation  in  his  ashram  on  the  banks  of  River  Saraswati,  Sage  Vyasa
saw  the  sufferings  of  all  in  Kali  Yuga  and  worried  for  their  welfare.  Feeling
dissatisﬁed  with  all  his  works  thus  far  (composition  and  compilation  of  other
Puranas, Mahabharata, etc.), he wondered what else he could do to help the people
of Kali Yuga and felt restless.

Chapter 05 & 06
Sage Narada arrived at Sri Vyasa’s ashram and was duly worshipped and welcomed
by Sage Vyasa. When Sage Vyasa asked Sage Narada to discern why he was feeling
restless,  Sage  Narada  pointed  out  that  he  still  needed  to  compose  a  scripture
describing the glories and leelas of Bhagavan. Sage Narada told the story of his own
past  birth  to  inspire  Sage  Vyasa  to  compose  a  scripture  which,  in  its  turn,  would
inspire devotion to Bhagavan in the hearts of listeners.

Narada Maharishi’s Story:
In his previous birth, born as a son of a maid in a village, Sage Narada was blessed
with the company of sadhus who had camped in his village during the 4 months of
rainy  season  having  undertaken  the  chaturmasya  vratha.  Being  in  their  constant
company,  his  ears  were  ﬁlled  with  the  glories  of  Bhagavan  which  they  discussed

the Lord and remembering lovingly His pastimes. When the time for his last breath
came,  his  body  fell,  and  the  child  was  later  reborn  as  Narada,  the  son  of  Lord

Vyasa saluted Sage Narada.

Chapter 07
After Sage Narada left, Sage Vyasa sat in meditation. In the state of samādhi he saw
all the divine pastimes of Bhagavan. Knowing that devotion to Krishna is the only
easy means for the people of Kali Yuga to attain ultimate good, he composed Srimad
Bhagavatam  -  a  scripture  that  would  instill  and  inspire  love  for  Bhagavan  in  the
hearts of listeners. He taught this to his son Sri Shuka who, though he was a self-
realised  soul  who  was  always  reveling  in  His  own  Self,  was  enamoured  by  the
divine leelas and glories of Bhagavan.
