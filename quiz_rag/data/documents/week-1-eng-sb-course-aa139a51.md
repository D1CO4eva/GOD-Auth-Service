1.1 Sanatana Dharma
‘Hinduism’ is a name given to Sanatana Dharma by western researchers. Our
religion originally had no name because, initially, it was the only one that existed.
Even the term ‘Sanatana Dharma’ is not an official name for our religion but it is a
term that means ‘eternal dharma’, because it is a dharma that always existed.
How did our religion then come to be called as Hinduism? According to historians,
every civilisation has grown along the banks of a river because people subsisted on
agriculture and maintenance of cattle. History refers to the civilisation that existed
along the banks of river Sindhu as ‘Indus Valley Civilisation’, ‘Indus’ being a later
variation of the name ‘Sindhu’. The people who lived along the river Sindhu were at
first called Sindhus. Over a period of time, the term morphed into Hindus, and thus
the term Hinduism came about.
With so much discussion on this term ‘Sanatana Dharma’, it is important that we as
Hindus understand the ‘literal’ meaning of Sanatana Dharma:
‘Sanātana'
-
traces its roots as ‘सदा भव’ (sadā bhava), which, after Samskrita grammar
treatments, becomes ‘Sanātana’.
-
Sanātana’ means that which exists (bhava) always (sadā); eternal, ever-present,
permanent, etc.
‘Dharma’
-
traces its root to the root verb ‘dhr’ meaning that which upholds, supports, or
maintains everything.
-
‘Dharma’ can also mean ‘an essential quality’ or ‘true innate nature’ of an entity.

1.2 Our Grand Shastras - Overview
• The authoritative texts which establish the true knowledge of Dharma are called
“Pramāṇas”. (Pramā means truth "as it is"; that which establishes this is
Pramāṇa.)
• The primary pramāṇa, supreme authority, are the "Vedas" (four branches). Apart
from the Vedas, there are 10 more texts which expand or supplement the Vedas,
making it 14.
• Add with appendices (four more), we have totally 18 first level Pramāṇas!
(These shall be discussed in the "Intermediate Level” course.)
Vedas (Means “Knowledge")
• This is considered to be the divine knowledge of Bhagavan Himself and
hence is the "root" of all creation.
• They are truly "infinite" — says the Vedas themselves.
• They are the “life breath” of Bhagavan.
• Bhagavan said in Srimad Bhagavad Gita: "I am known through all the Vedas.-
(vEdaishcha sarvai: ahameva vedya: -BG 15:15)
• A great Rishi (Āpastamba) said: "Vedas are the Pramāṇa of all Dharma."
Vedas are not a product of human intelligence! (Sanskrit term: Apauruṣheya)
• The pronounced sound of the Vedas is the key! The sound itself has creative
ability. It also encompasses grand divine meaning covering all facets of life.
If at all we need to point to one rishi in history as a rejuvenator of our Dharma, it is
Sage Veda Vyāsa (The divine incarnation of Bhagavan’s “Jnana”, born to Sage
Parāshara & Satyavati Devi)
Sage Veda Vyāsa codified the Vedas into 4 branches:
1. Ṛig — "Hymn in praise.” (Riks)
2. Yajur — "Worship with sacrifice"; ritualistic procedures.
3. Sāma — Means "peace to minds”; Riks set to music.
4. Atharva — Types of mantras to ward off evil/hardship (discovered by Rishi
named Atharvan)

1.3 Place of Itihasas & Puranas in Shastras

1. Srimad Rāmāyanam (24,000 slokas by Sri Valmiki Maharshi)

2. Sriman Mahābhāratam (100,000+ slokas by Sri Veda Vyasa Maharshi)
