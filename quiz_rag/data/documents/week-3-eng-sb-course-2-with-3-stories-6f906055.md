3.1  Being a Rasika

Here is a secret!
A Purana story can be embellished in three deeper levels
a) Dharma (Righteousness / Do’s & Dont’s / the underlying value)
b) Tattva (The underlying philosophical principle/tenet)
c) Rasa (The relish / aesthetic ﬂavor expressed ‘poetically’)
Great ones generally tell stories to all; dharma to thousand; tattva to ten and rasa to
one!
Srimad Bhagavatam is a rare Purana that is almost exclusively for Rasikas.
Let us be a Rasika by divine grace, to relish this journey the most!

Let us see these three narratives with reference to Srimad Bhagavata Mahatmyam
itself as an example:
a) Dharma narrative
“Listen  to  and  read  Srimad  Bhagavatam;  it  is  enough!  Chant  the  divine  name  of
Hari and be in a satsang as much possible. Four major Puranas speak extensively
about  the  greatness  (Mahatmya)  of  the  Srimad  Bhagavatam:  the  Skanda  Purana,
Padma Purana, Garuda Purana, and Naradiya Purana.”

b) Tattva narrative (takes a little more maturity from listener)
“Srimad  Bhagavatam  is  the  essence  of  all  Puranas.  Listening  to  other  Puranas
should  gravitate  the  aspirant  towards  Srimad  Bhagavatam,  as  it  is  summum
bonnum of learning. As seen above, four major Puranas speak extensively about the
greatness (Mahatmya) of the Srimad Bhagavatam. Why?”
Skanda Purana, which extols the glory of holy pilgrimage places (tīrtha);
Padma Purana, which celebrates the greatness of religious vows (vrathas);

‘Purana Tilakam’!”

3.2 Srimad Bhagavata Mahatmyam

The most extensive and popular narration of the greatness of Srimad Bhagavatam is
in the Padma Purana in its Uttara Khanda, where it is extolled in six chapters.

वेदोपिनषदां सारात् जाता भागवती कथा । अत्युत्तमा ततो भाित पृथग्भूता फलाकृ ित:
(Mahatmya 2.67)
The  story  of  Srimad  Bhagavatam  has  emanated  from  the  essence  of  the  Vedas  and  the
Upanishads.  Having  an  existence  apart  from  them  and  representing  their  (very)  fruit  (as  it
were), it appears to be the very best.

3.3  Four Stories to the Fore

Padma  Purana  has  four  stories  and  many  details  on  the  greatness  of  Srimad
Bhagavatam:
1. Parikshith & Nectar episode
2. Brahmaji & ‘The Measuring’ episode
3. Narada Maharishi & Bhakti Devi episode
4. Atma Deva & Dhundhukari episode
Each  of  these  episodes  have  been  narrated  by  Veda  Vyasa  Bhagavan  to  show
various aspects of Srimad Bhagavatam’s greatness in a Rasa narrative backed up by
its Tattva and the Dharma narratives.

Appendix:
Read the stories in detail here.
1. Parikshith & Nectar episode
When  Shuka  Maharishi  took  his  seat  in  the  assembly  of  sages,  to  give  Srimad
Bhagavatam  to  Parikshith  Maharaja,  Devas  arrived  there  with  a  bowl  of  amruta
(nectar). They prostrated to Shuka Maharishi and prayed, “Let Parikshith have this
bowl of nectar (amruta), and we shall have the nectar of Srimad Bhagavata.” Shuka
Maharishi  merely  laughed  thinking,  “This  amruta  of  Devas  is  incomparable  to
Srimad  Bhagavata  amruta.  This  is  like  comparing  a  piece  of  glass  to  a  precious
gem.”  Knowing  thus  and  that  Devas  were  not  devotees,  Shuka  Maharishi  did  not
give them the nectar in the form of Srimad Bhagavatam.

Purport of this episode (Tattva):
Generally  a  word  may  indicate  various  meanings  depending  on  the  context.  E.g
Amruta (Nectar) can mean the tasty divine drink in Devaloka sanctioned by Indra
for  the  new  entrants.  This  makes  them  revel  in  pleasures  of  heaven  for  quite  a
while!  Amruta  can  also  mean  ‘a-mruta’  or  conquering  death  (deathlessness),
signifying moksha or the state of fear-less bliss. This episode is narrated at the very
outset  to  clarify  that  Deva  amruta  is  nowhere  close  in  comparison  to  Srimad
Bhagavata amruta - in fact Srimad Bhagavata is the highest gift of Nectar to a select
few who truly understand its greatness. Even heavenly beings are not fortunate to
qualify for this nectar. So, Veda Vyasa Bhagavan is telling us the magnitude of our
fortune!

2. Brahmaji & the Measuring episode
Brahmaji was stunned to witness Parikshith’s emancipation as a result of listening
to Srimad Bhagavatam. In Satyaloka, he set up a weighing balance and placed all
the  divine  scriptures  on  one  side  of  the  balance  and  Srimad  Bhagavatam  on  the
other side. The plate with all other scriptures weighed less and the other side with
Srimad  Bhagavatam  proved  to  be  superior.  All  the  Rishis  who  witnessed  this
wonderful sight regarded Srimad Bhagavatam as Bhagavan Himself and capable of
granting the speedy access to Srivaikuntha, when read or listened to.

3. Narada Maharishi & Bhakti Devi episode
Once, the great Kumaras (Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanatana, Sanatkumara), the mind-
born  sons  of  Brahma,  arrived  in  Badrikashrama  seeking  satsang.  There  they
chanced to meet Sage Narada who looked very dejected. On enquiring why he was
so, Sage Narada described his experience of visiting Earth.

Sage  Narada  said,  “Having  visited  the  most  sacred  kshetras  (holy  places  of
pilgrimage)  and  tīrthas  (holy  rivers),  I  was  pained  and  shocked  to  see  the  state  of
affairs  on  Earth.  The  effects  of  Kali  Yuga  are  on  display  everywhere.  There  is  no
truth, kindness, compassion, devotion, or selﬂessness. There is not one yogi or jnāni
or  siddha  to  be  found  anywhere.  Everyone’s  focus  is  on  caring  for  nothing  but
themselves  selﬁshly.  Having  seen  such  evils  of  Kali  Yuga,  I  visited  the  banks  of
river Yamuna where Bhagavan had performed innumerable leelas.

“There was a wonderful scene! There sat a young woman who seemed distressed,
and  by  her  side  lay  two  old  men  who  were  unconscious.  She  was  trying  to  bring
them  back  to  consciousness  while  she  herself  was  being  fanned  by  hundreds  of
women.  Seeing  this  from  a  distance,  I  approached  her  out  of  curiosity.  On  seeing
me, she immediately got up and said, ‘O Sage! Do halt! Put an end to my worry!
Your very sight would relieve one of all afﬂictions!’ I for my part enquired, ‘Who

“Moved by the appeal of a mother, I tried to awaken Jnāna & Vairāgya. I chanted
Upanishads  and  Bhagavad  Gita.  Hearing  these  they  arose  slightly  with  great
exertion  but  were  exhausted  and  were  inclined  to  fall  back  into  sleep  again.
Astonished to see this, I pondered as to what else I could do to awaken them from
their  slumber.  While  I  was  pondering  thus,  a  celestial  voice  was  heard  from  the
heavens  -  ‘Do  not  feel  dejected,  O  Sage!  You  have  to  perform  a  ‘satkarma’  (a
righteous  act).  Saints  will  point  this  act  out  to  you.  When  that  noble  act  is  done
Jnāna & Vairāgya will wake up and their old age will be gone too.’”

Having  narrated  this  whole  story,  Sage  Narada  continued,  “Hence  I  started  the
journey  from  there  in  search  of  saints  who  would  enlighten  me  on  this  satkarma.
Deciding to practice penance, I reached Badarikashrama and now I am blessed to
have  met  you,  exalted  sages,  and  described  this  entire  experience  to  you.  Now
please enlighten me on what that satkarma is.”

The four Kumaras, who looked like mere children but were ancient and wise, said -
“Worry not O Sage! There exists an easy way to do this. Many paths are arduous
and yet bestow only the temporary swarga (heaven), and nothing more. The path to
Vaikunta is indeed a secret and it is by rare fortune that a person who knows that
path is found. We shall tell you the satkarma that the heavenly voice hinted at. It is
the jnāna yagna of reading of Srimad Bhagavata Mahapurana.
bhakti jnāna virāgānām tadgoshena balam mahat

the Vedas alone? Pray resolve this doubt of mine.”

The Kumaras replied, “Srimad Bhagavatam is indeed the essence of Vedas. But
- just as the essence of a tree, though existing all through, can be enjoyed only in
